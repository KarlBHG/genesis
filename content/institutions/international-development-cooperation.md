# International development cooperation

## Summary
The Ministry for Foreign Affairs says Iceland’s international development cooperation has a long history and remains one of the main pillars of Iceland’s foreign policy. By actively participating in development cooperation, Iceland seeks to reduce poverty and promote sustainable development, human rights, and gender equality.

Cooperation is guided by the **policy for international development cooperation for 2024–2028**, which supports the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals → [[un-2030-agenda]]. The page says ODA will increase during that policy period from **0.35% of GNI in 2024 to 0.46% in 2028**. It also records that in **2021**, contributions to official development assistance amounted to **71.9 million USD**, or **0.28% of GNI**, and that Iceland aims to increase contributions toward **0.35% of GNI**.

**Bilateral partner countries** on the page are Malawi, Sierra Leone, and Uganda.

**Multilateral emphasis** includes the World Bank, UNICEF, UN Women, and UNFPA; climate funds including the Green Climate Fund, Adaptation Fund, and Nordic Development Fund; and humanitarian bodies including CERF, OCHA, WFP, and UNHCR.

Expertise channels include land restoration, fisheries, geothermal energy, and the UNESCO-GRÓ Centre for Capacity Development, Sustainability and Societal Change.
