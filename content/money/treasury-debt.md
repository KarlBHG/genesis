# Treasury debt

## Summary
The Ministry of Finance and Economic Affairs is responsible for central-government debt management, formulates the strategy, and decides issuance. Government Debt Management, a department of the Central Bank of Iceland, executes that strategy under an agreement with the ministry → [[ministry-of-finance]] → [[sedlabanki]]. The stock below is the ministry’s Treasury figure at the end of November 2024, not a later outturn and not general-government debt.

## Outstanding stock
The Medium-Term Debt Management Strategy 2025–2029, issued 30 December 2024, says gross Treasury debt totalled ISK 1,924bn at the end of November 2024, equivalent to 42.3% of GDP. Table 1 in that strategy is Treasury Part A1 debt, net of pension obligations and accounts payable, at the same date. The table’s total liabilities line prints 1,924,177 million ISK.

The table splits that stock, in million ISK, into Treasury bills 202,921, index-linked Treasury bonds 462,936, nominal Treasury bonds 612,132, accrued unpaid interest 39,920, other domestic liabilities 269,740, domestic liabilities in total 1,587,649, EMTN issues 298,685, the loan for the acquisition of Landsnet 37,843 → [[landsnet]], and foreign liabilities in total 336,528. A later sentence in the same strategy says Treasury foreign debt amounted to ISK 343.2bn at the end of November 2024. The table and that sentence are both printed. They are not the same foreign total. The prose also describes the Landsnet loan as ISK 37.9bn, against the table’s 37,843 million ISK.

At the end of November 2024 the strategy classifies the debt as nominal Treasury bonds 32%, indexed Treasury bonds 25%, Treasury bills 11%, foreign debt 18%, and other non-marketable debt 14%. Separately, indexed debt adjusted for inflation swaps was ISK 730bn, about 39% of total debt. Of the outstanding loan balance at that date, adjusted for swap agreements, 89% bore fixed interest and about 11% bore variable interest or was short-term.

## Maturity
The strategy says the average time to maturity of total Treasury debt shall be at least five years and no longer than seven years. It also says the aim is to keep the weighted average time to maturity of Treasury bonds between five and seven years. Those are targets. The only average the text prints is for an earlier date: at the end of November 2023, the average time to maturity was 5.01 years. Figure 5 shows the redemption profile at the end of November 2024. The text does not print an average maturity for that date.

## Who manages it
The strategy says the Act on Public Finances, No. 123/2015, and the Act on Government Debt Management, No. 43/1990, make the ministry responsible. In each year’s national budget, Parliament authorises the ministry to borrow and to issue state guarantees. The ministry’s agreement with the Central Bank covers advice and execution. Government Debt Management publishes the ratings and the debt statistics on lanamal.is. The 2026 budget law’s borrowing caps are authorisations for that year, recorded on the budget page, not this stock → [[althingi]] → [[treasury-budget-2026]].

A ministry release of 19 December 2025 says Treasury debt is projected to be essentially unchanged as a share of GDP, at 37.4% at year-end 2026. That is a projection in the release. It is not the November 2024 stock.

## Credit ratings
The Government Debt Management ratings page says two companies rate the Icelandic government: Moody’s Investors Service and Standard & Poor’s. Its table prints Moody’s, last change September 2024, at A1 long-term foreign and A1 long-term domestic, outlook Stable. Short-term cells for Moody’s are empty on that table. Standard & Poor’s, last change March 2026, is printed as A+ long-term foreign, A-1 short-term foreign, A+ long-term domestic, A-1 short-term domestic, outlook Positive.

The same page says that on 6 February 2026 Fitch Ratings upgraded Iceland’s long-term foreign-currency issuer default rating to A+ from A, with a stable outlook, and subsequently withdrew the ratings for commercial reasons. An older item on that page, 15 February 2025, says Fitch affirmed the rating at A. The February 2026 sentence is the later statement on the page. The Government of Iceland debt-management page still says three agencies assign Iceland a rating: Fitch, Moody’s, and Standard & Poor’s. That page does not print the rating symbols. Both pages are quoted. The symbols above are the ones the debt-management ratings table prints.
