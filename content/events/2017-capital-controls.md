## Summary
On 12 March 2017 the Government of Iceland announced that capital controls on individuals, firms, and pension funds had been lifted, with effect on Tuesday. The release does not print that Tuesday's date. It says the liberalisation is laid down in new foreign-exchange rules issued by the Central Bank, and that individuals, companies, and pension funds can invest abroad without restrictions. The same release says capital flows are unrestricted apart from restrictions on carry trade and on derivatives denominated in krónur, and that offshore króna holders who neither took part in Central Bank auctions nor took part in the agreement announced that day would not be able to move their assets. It is not a statement that every control had been removed. The 2008 authorisation is [[2008-capital-controls]]. The Bank is [[sedlabanki]].
