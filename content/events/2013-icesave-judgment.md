## Summary
On 28 January 2013 the EFTA Court delivered judgment in Case E-16/11, EFTA Surveillance Authority v Iceland. The Court's case page says the application was submitted on 15 December 2011 and heard on 18 September 2012. The judgment says the Authority sought a declaration that Iceland had failed to ensure payment of the minimum compensation to Icesave depositors in the Netherlands and the United Kingdom under Directive 94/19/EC, and had failed to comply with that Directive and/or Article 4 of the EEA Agreement. The Court dismissed the application. It held that, by that failure to ensure payment, Iceland had not failed to comply with the obligations resulting from the Directive, in particular Articles 3, 4, 7 and 10, and/or Article 4 of the EEA Agreement. It ordered the Authority to pay its own costs and the costs incurred by Iceland, and ordered the European Commission to bear its own costs. The EFTA entry is [[efta]]. The EEA Agreement is [[eea-agreement]]. The referendums are [[2011-icesave-referendum]].
