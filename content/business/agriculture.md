# Agriculture

## Summary
The Ministry of Industries says it is responsible for agriculture, aquaculture, fisheries, food, and food safety. Its organisation chart places agriculture, food safety, and food in the Department of Land Quality and Animal Health, and names the Icelandic Food and Veterinary Authority (MAST) and Matís among the bodies that department coordinates. Fisheries and the Directorate of Fisheries sit in a different department of the same ministry → [[ministry-of-industries]] → [[fisheries-and-quotas]].

What leaves the country as agricultural goods is small next to what comes in as food. In 2024, agricultural products exports were 8.8 billion ISK fob, 0.9% of goods exports → [[agricultural-products-exports-2024]]. Food and beverages imports were 121.7 billion ISK cif, 9.0% of goods imports → [[food-and-beverages-imports-2024]]. Statistics Iceland publishes those as different rows. This page does not relabel the import row as agricultural products.

Home production of meat, from the meat-production chart on Statistics Iceland’s agriculture production page, in tonnes for 2024: mutton and lamb 7,966; beef 4,860; horse meat 982; pork 6,756; poultry 10,079. The same chart continues through 2025. Those later tonnes are not repeated here, because the trade figures in this map are for 2024. The chart does not give a króna value for that meat.

On trade with the EU, the Ministry for Foreign Affairs does not describe fisheries and agriculture as simply outside the EEA. It says the EEA Agreement eliminates tariffs on industrial products originating in a party. Protocol 3 liberalises tariffs on processed agricultural products. Protocol 9 liberalises tariffs on fish products. Unprocessed agricultural products are handled by a separate 2018 bilateral agreement with the EU, which replaced a 2007 agreement. Protocol 6 to the 1972 Iceland–EEC free-trade agreement still applies to fish products where it gives better access than Protocol 9 → [[eea-agreement]].
